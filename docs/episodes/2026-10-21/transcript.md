# Omaha Morning Brief

_Wednesday, October 21, 2026_

## The short version

Good morning, Warren. It is Wednesday, October 21st. Housing is buckling under higher mortgage rates: signed contracts to buy homes fell to a record low. Companies, meanwhile, reported strong quarters. General Motors approved a $20 billion buyback, and three big defense and aerospace makers raised their outlooks.

## Rates and inflation

The 10-year Treasury yield eased to 5.12% after the weak housing data. Three-month Treasury bills pay 4.26%. Futures still put the odds of a Federal Reserve rate increase on October 28 at roughly one in six, and see one by December as likely.

Pending home sales, which count signed contracts, fell 7.3% in September to the lowest level since the series began in 2001. Economists expected a small drop. The Realtors' chief economist, Lawrence Yun, blamed fast-rising mortgage rates. Housing starts fell 5.2% to an annual rate of 1.22 million, well below forecasts, and permits for single-family homes fell 4%.

## Markets

The Dow rose 1% to 52,496, led by 3M. The S&P 500 rose 0.3% to 7,713, and health care stocks reached a record. The Nasdaq was flat as money moved out of technology and into industrial companies and smaller companies.

Brent crude touched $112 during the day, then settled down 0.7% at $108.79 on hopes for talks between the United States and Iran.

## Companies

General Motors earned $3.70 a share, above forecasts, on record revenue of $50 billion. Its board approved a new $20 billion buyback, after it finished a $6 billion one. Shares outstanding are down about 6% since the end of last year. But the stock fell about 6%. Chief financial officer Paul Jacobson said steel, aluminum and shipping costs rose sharply in the last two months, and could cost about $2 billion next year if they stay high.

Netflix earned 87 cents a share against 82 expected, with an operating margin of 34.6%, up from 28.2% a year ago. But it cut its advertising revenue forecast for the year to about $2.7 billion from about $3 billion, and the shares fell after hours.

Defense and aerospace demand is strong. RTX raised its profit and sales outlook, with a record backlog of $340 billion. Northrop Grumman also raised its outlook, with a record backlog near $110 billion. GE Aerospace raised its forecast for the second time this year, but its shares fell because cash flow was lighter than expected.

3M rose 6% after beating forecasts and raising its outlook. Amazon agreed to buy up to $6 billion of 3M's data center connectors over seven years, and in return can buy up to about 3.2 million 3M shares.

SpaceX reported its first quarterly profit since going public, $4.4 billion, on revenue of $14.6 billion. Its artificial intelligence business is now larger than Starlink.

In deals, Salesforce agreed to buy Braze, a marketing software company, for about $5.7 billion in cash, a 58% premium.

## The world

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on Tuesday that mines stopped more tankers south of the Strait of Hormuz. The United States military called the claims false and said the shipping lanes are clear of mines. Tanker traffic through the strait remains a small fraction of normal, and a United States official said the blockade of Iranian ports stays in full force.

Saudi Arabia postponed its Future Investment Initiative conference, planned for next week in Riyadh, to 2027. The organizers did not give security as a reason, but it follows Houthi attacks on Saudi targets, including a strike on Riyadh's airport this month that killed at least 12 people.

In France, the 10-year government bond yield rose again to near 5%, its highest in about 24 years, as more protests are planned for Saturday.

## Coming up

Today, Britain reports September inflation, expected to rise to about 3.4%. After the New York close, Tesla, Meta and IBM report. On Thursday, Procter & Gamble and Lockheed Martin report, along with weekly jobless claims. On Friday, American Express reports, Japan publishes consumer prices, and the University of Michigan releases its final October survey. The Federal Reserve decides next Wednesday. That is the briefing. Have a good day.
