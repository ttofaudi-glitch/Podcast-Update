# Omaha Morning Brief

_Tuesday, November 3, 2026_

## The short version

Good morning, Warren. It is Tuesday, November 3rd. Oil had its largest one-day fall since July as Iran and the United States kept talking through mediators. Apple and Palantir both reported results above estimates after the close. Marvell agreed to buy Lumentum in an all-stock deal valued at $115 billion. Americans vote in the midterm elections today.

## Rates and inflation

The 10-year Treasury yield fell to 4.91%, and the 30-year to 5.28%. Shorter yields rose: the 2-year to 4.59%, and the 3-month bill to 4.21%.

The Institute for Supply Management's factory index rose to 55.5 in October, above the 54.5 expected and the tenth month of expansion. Production reached its highest level since November 2021. The prices-paid gauge eased to 75.2 from 77.9, which is still high. Futures markets put the odds of a Federal Reserve rate increase in December at about three in four.

The Treasury expects to borrow $618 billion this quarter and $800 billion in the first quarter of next year. It sets the size of its debt sales on Wednesday.

## Markets

Investors moved out of technology and into defensive stocks. The Dow rose 0.41%. The S&P 500 fell 0.51%, and the Nasdaq fell 1.07%. Chip stocks fell for a second day. Oracle fell more than 8% after filing to sell $10 billion of new stock and $10 billion of bonds to pay for artificial intelligence data centers. Its credit rating is one step above junk.

Brent crude settled at $104.77 a barrel, down 7.6%. West Texas Intermediate settled at $96.46. Goldman Sachs raised its December Brent forecast to $105.

## Companies

Apple reported revenue of $114.5 billion for its September quarter, up 12% and a record for that quarter. iPhone sales rose about 17%, to $57.6 billion. Services reached a record $31.6 billion. Earnings were $2.10 a share, above estimates, but about 5 cents came from a one-time tariff refund. The gross margin was 47.9%, and the refund added about one point of that. Chief executive John Ternus said memory chips cost more. Sales in Greater China missed estimates. The shares were little changed to slightly lower after hours.

Palantir's revenue rose 96%, to $2.31 billion. Sales to American companies, up 148%, were larger than its American government business for the first time. It raised its full-year forecast to about $8.53 billion. The shares moved only slightly after hours.

Marvell will pay 4.5 of its shares for each Lumentum share, a 24% premium. Marvell holders will own about two-thirds of the combined company. Marvell shares fell 12.7%.

Diamondback Energy raised its base dividend 7.3% and its oil production forecast. IDEXX rose 22% on higher sales and a raised outlook.

## The world

Iran's negotiators left Doha on Sunday with no deal. Iran has offered a two-day plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz if the United States lifts its naval blockade, and is waiting for an answer. Qatari mediators flew to Tehran on Monday night. President Trump said the war would end soon, one way or the other. The Wall Street Journal reported that Gulf allies were told to prepare for possible American strikes after today's vote. Iran's claims that two tankers hit mines have not been confirmed.

In the midterms, forecasters favor Democrats to win the House. The Senate is closer: Democrats need a net gain of four seats. Analysts treat divided government as the base case.

Ukrainian drones hit three Russian refineries in three nights, about a tenth of Russia's refining capacity.

A correction: yesterday we said Israel's President Herzog begins consultations on Wednesday. Wednesday is when he formally receives the election results. The new parliament is sworn in on November 10th.

## Coming up

Australia's central bank decides today and is expected to raise its rate to 4.85%. France's National Assembly holds a key budget vote, and the government's survival may depend on it. Pfizer and Shopify report today before the open; AMD, Amgen and Arista after the close. Wednesday brings private payrolls, the services survey and Uber. On Thursday, the Bank of England is expected to raise its rate to 4%. Friday's jobs report is expected to show about 60,000 new jobs. That is the briefing. Have a good day.
