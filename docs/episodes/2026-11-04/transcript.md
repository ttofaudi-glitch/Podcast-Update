# Omaha Morning Brief

_Wednesday, November 4, 2026_

## The short version

Good morning, Warren. It is Wednesday, November 4th. Americans voted in the midterm elections yesterday, and no results had been called when this briefing was made. American forces carried out limited strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, while talks continue. AMD reported results well above estimates. Apple fell after warning that memory chip costs will squeeze its margins.

## Rates and inflation

The 10-year Treasury yield rose to 4.95%, and the 2-year to 4.61%. The 3-month bill was unchanged at 4.21%.

Employers posted 7.5 million job openings in September, more than the 7.1 million expected. The share of workers quitting rose to 2.1%, the highest in 18 months. People quit more when they are confident of finding new work. Factory orders rose 1.8% in September, also above forecasts. Futures markets still put the odds of a Federal Reserve rate increase in December at about three in four.

Australia's central bank raised its rate to 4.85%, the highest since 2008. The vote was six to three, and it dropped its earlier warning of further increases.

## Markets

The S&P 500 rose 0.36%, ending a three-day fall. The Nasdaq rose 0.83% as chip stocks rebounded nearly 3%. The Dow fell 0.38%, held back by Apple.

Brent crude settled at $103.60 a barrel, down 1.1% and its lowest close in about four weeks. West Texas Intermediate settled at $96.09. Prices rose early on the strikes on Iran, then eased on hopes for a deal.

## Companies

AMD's revenue rose 45%, to $13.4 billion, above estimates. Its data center sales nearly doubled, to $8.5 billion. Adjusted earnings were $2.09 a share, against about $1.95 expected. Chief executive Lisa Su said growth will speed up this quarter. The shares rose about 7% after hours. Arista Networks and Super Micro Computer also beat estimates and gave forecasts above expectations.

Apple fell about 2%. It expects its gross margin to fall to between 45% and 46% this quarter, from 47.9%, because memory chips cost more. Analysts had expected about 46.3%. It expects sales to grow 7% to 10%. Chief executive John Ternus said memory prices will keep rising beyond December. Palantir fell 1.65% despite strong results.

Marathon Petroleum earned far more than expected as refining margins widened. It returned $3.3 billion to shareholders in the quarter, raised its dividend 10%, added $5 billion to its buyback, and agreed to sell two refineries to CVR Energy for about $4.2 billion. Pfizer raised its full-year forecast. Shopify fell about 7% after the value of goods sold through its platform missed estimates. Booking Holdings said hotel bookings will slow sharply this quarter.

## The world

American officials said the strikes on Qeshm Island were defensive, after Iran's Revolutionary Guard fired at an American aircraft, and do not mean a return to full combat. Qatari mediators were in Tehran discussing a draft deal. Iranian officials called the American response to their demands unconvincing. Vice President Vance said there had been some progress.

The United States has announced three-way peace talks with Russia and Ukraine in Abu Dhabi, starting November 10th.

Three corrections to yesterday's briefing. France's budget vote did not take place. It was postponed again because hundreds of amendments remain. Our figure that Ukraine had hit about a tenth of Russia's refining capacity came from open-source analysts and is not confirmed. And Israel's President Herzog receives the election results today and is expected to begin talks with the parties this evening, so yesterday's correction on that point was wrong.

## Coming up

Election results will come in through the day, and some close races may take days. Republicans hold the Senate 53 to 47, so Democrats need a net gain of four seats to take control. Today brings private payrolls, the services survey, and the Treasury's plan for its debt sales. McDonald's and Uber report before the open, and Qualcomm and Arm after the close. On Thursday, the Bank of England is expected to raise its rate to 4%. Friday's jobs report is expected to show about 60,000 new jobs. That is the briefing. Have a good day.
