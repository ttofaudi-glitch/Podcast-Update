# Omaha Morning Brief

_Monday, October 26, 2026_

## The short version

Good morning, Warren. It is Monday, October 26th. Oil ended last week at its highest price since April, because of Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia and the risk of new American strikes on Iran. This is a heavy week: the Federal Reserve, the European Central Bank and the Bank of Japan all decide, and Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon report.

## Rates and inflation

The 10-year Treasury yield ended Friday at 5.00%, down from 5.09% a week earlier. Three-month Treasury bills pay 4.22%. Futures put the odds of a Federal Reserve rate increase on Wednesday at about one in ten, and see an increase in December as more likely than not.

Price pressure is still building. A survey of American businesses by S&P Global showed selling prices rising at the fastest pace since August 2022. The University of Michigan's consumer sentiment index ended October at 48, near record lows, and households expect 4.7% inflation over the next year.

In Japan, core consumer prices rose 2.1% in September, above the Bank of Japan's 2% target for the first time in nine months. The Bank of Japan is expected to hold its rate at 1.25% on Friday, with an increase in December possible.

## Markets

On Friday the Dow fell 1.2% to 52,215, and the S&P 500 fell 0.5% to 7,624. The Nasdaq slipped 0.2%. For the week, the S&P lost 0.5% and the Nasdaq 0.7%, while the Dow gained 0.4%, its third weekly gain in a row.

Brent crude settled at $116.72, up 3.1% on the day and about 6% for the week, its highest close since late April. West Texas Intermediate closed at $103.27. Energy was the only clear winner among stock sectors.

## Companies

American Express earned $4.68 a share on an adjusted basis, above the $4.49 expected, on revenue of $20.1 billion, up 9%. Its net write-off rate fell to 1.8% from 1.9% a year ago, so card credit is holding up. It raised its full-year profit outlook. But it will cut about 5% of its workforce, roughly 3,500 jobs, and took a $336 million charge. The chief financial officer said the cuts remove management layers and prepare for automation and artificial intelligence. The stock fell about 3%.

Telephone companies fell after Bloomberg reported that SpaceX is negotiating access to at least 80,000 American cell sites for its Starlink mobile service. AT&T and T-Mobile fell sharply, and tower owner Crown Castle rose. These are talks, not a deal.

On Saturday, President Trump announced that Audi will build its first American factory, near Macon, Georgia. It will cost about $4 billion and employ about 4,500 people, with production starting in the early 2030s.

## The world

On Sunday, President Trump said there would be no strike on Iran that day, and that he is weighing a deal against further military action. Qatari mediators were in Tehran on Sunday, in an effort coordinated with the United States. Iran's parliament speaker warned of a strong response to any attack. Iran says it would reopen the Strait of Hormuz if the American naval blockade is lifted.

The Houthis claimed new attacks on Saudi oil facilities on Saturday, including Yanbu. Saudi forces said they intercepted the missiles, and no damage has been confirmed.

Moody's kept France's credit rating at Aa3 with a negative outlook on Friday, avoiding the downgrade many investors expected. It said the rating depends on France passing a budget that cuts the deficit, with debt at about 119% of gross domestic product. Hundreds of thousands of people protested across France on Saturday.

## Coming up

Verizon reports today. Tuesday brings Coca-Cola, Boeing and Visa. On Wednesday, the Federal Reserve decides, the Bank of Canada decides, Britain presents its budget, and Microsoft and Alphabet report after the close. Thursday brings the first estimate of third-quarter gross domestic product, September inflation, the European Central Bank, and reports from Eli Lilly, Caterpillar, Amazon and Intel. On Friday, the Bank of Japan decides, and Exxon and Chevron report. Apple reports next Monday. That is the briefing. Have a good day.
