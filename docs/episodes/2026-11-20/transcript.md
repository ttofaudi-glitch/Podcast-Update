# Omaha Morning Brief

_Friday, November 20, 2026_

## The short version

Good morning, Warren. It is Friday, November 20th. Stocks fell about 2% across the board, and the Dow closed below 50,000 for the first time since June. Anthropic, which makes the system that writes this briefing, rose 17% on its first day of trading. Walmart raised its outlook again. And in Shenzhen, Presidents Xi, Trump and Putin met and issued a short declaration, with no breakthroughs.

## Rates and inflation

Weekly jobless claims rose to 203,000, slightly above forecasts, and continuing claims reached their highest since late August. The Philadelphia Federal Reserve Bank's factory index fell to 6.3 from 20.9, its lowest since May, while its prices-paid gauge rose.

Futures markets give about nine in ten odds of a quarter-point increase in December, with the rest on half a point. Governor Christopher Waller has argued for a larger move or a clear signal of more to come.

The 10-year Treasury yield eased to 5.04%, and the 2-year to 4.76%. The 3-month bill pays 4.34%. The yen stayed just under 160 to the dollar.

## Markets

The Dow fell 1,101 points, or 2.19%, to 49,097. The S&P 500 fell 2.02%, to 7,176, its sixth straight loss, and the Nasdaq fell 2.25%. Federal Reserve warnings about inflation and fading enthusiasm for artificial intelligence stocks outweighed the Anthropic debut. Nvidia fell about 3%, its second fall since its results.

Oil was little changed, with Brent near $92 a barrel, as tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz were offset by rising American inventories. Brent is down about a fifth this month. Gold fell again, to about $3,700 an ounce, its lowest since September 2025.

## Companies

Anthropic priced its shares at $83 and opened at $111.50. It closed at $97.17, up 17%, valued at well over a trillion dollars. It is one of the largest share sales on record.

Walmart's adjusted earnings were 65 cents a share, a cent above estimates, on revenue of $189 billion. Online sales in America rose 23%. It raised its full-year forecast for the third time, but the sales outlook was below some analysts' hopes, and the shares fell about 1%.

After the close, Intuit fell about 13% on a softer outlook. It agreed to sell Mailchimp for about $3.7 billion and bought back $1.5 billion of stock in the quarter. Ross Stores fell about 9% after hours, despite beating estimates, because its holiday quarter forecast was below expectations. Williams-Sonoma fell about 15% on weak sales.

## The world

In Shenzhen, Presidents Xi, Trump and Putin met for more than an hour. Their four-point declaration reaffirmed that a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought, and pledged dialogue on reducing nuclear risks. It said nothing about the war in Ukraine. President Xi called the meeting fruitful; American analysts said it produced little of substance. Separate talks between Presidents Trump and Xi did little on the trade truce, which expires in January. China says President Trump told President Xi that America does not support Taiwan independence; the White House has not confirmed that. The Kremlin says Presidents Trump and Putin spent about 40 minutes on Ukraine, with nothing announced, and President Putin said the sides are not yet agreeing to compromises. All 21 Asia-Pacific economies adopted a joint statement on digital trade, artificial intelligence and supply chains.

Iran said two more supertankers hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz, which is not independently confirmed. At the climate talks in Turkey, the latest draft deal dropped all references to fossil fuels, and several countries still object.

## Next week

A short week in New York. Medtronic, Best Buy and Analog Devices report on Tuesday morning, and Dell, HP and Workday that evening. On Wednesday, a heavy day: revised third-quarter growth, October inflation as measured by the Federal Reserve's preferred gauge, durable goods orders and weekly jobless claims, and Deere's results. American markets close on Thursday for Thanksgiving and close early on Friday. The Bank of Korea decides on rates on Thursday. That is the briefing. Have a good day.
