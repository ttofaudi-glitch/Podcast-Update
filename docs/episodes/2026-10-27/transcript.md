# Omaha Morning Brief

_Tuesday, October 27, 2026_

## The short version

Good morning, Warren. It is Tuesday, October 27th. Stocks rose on Monday after the artificial intelligence company Anthropic filed to go public. Meta announced plans to raise up to $95 billion by selling stock. Oil eased as Saudi, Pakistani and Turkish officials met Iran's leaders in Tehran. And Brazil's markets fell sharply after President Lula won re-election.

## Rates and inflation

The 10-year Treasury yield was little changed, near 4.99%. The 2-year rose to about 4.72%. Three-month Treasury bills pay about 4.2%. The Federal Reserve begins its meeting today and decides on Wednesday. Futures put the odds of a rate increase this week at under one in ten, and about three in four for an increase by December.

Gasoline averages about $4.29 a gallon, against $3.05 a year ago. By AAA's figures, the average price during President Trump's second term has now edged past the average under President Biden, a week before the midterm elections. The first estimate of third-quarter gross domestic product and the September inflation figures come out on Thursday.

## Markets

The S&P 500 rose 0.7% to 7,677, and the Nasdaq rose 0.8%. The Dow rose 0.3%. Chip stocks rose about 3%.

Brent crude settled at $115.81, down 0.8%, after briefly topping $117. West Texas Intermediate fell 1.5% to $101.76.

## Companies

Anthropic filed its prospectus to list on the Nasdaq under the ticker A N T. It reported revenue of about $16.3 billion for the first half of this year, against about $1.2 billion a year earlier. It also reported a net loss of $80.7 billion, most of it an accounting charge that does not involve cash. The price and the number of shares are not yet set. Note that Anthropic makes the system that writes this briefing.

Meta plans to sell $35 billion of stock and convertible preferred shares now, and up to $60 billion more later, to pay for its artificial intelligence build-out. Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom have said they may buy up to $5 billion. Meta's stock fell about 2%. The new shares would dilute existing owners. Last quarter, Meta's capital spending exceeded the cash its business produced.

EQT agreed to buy Range Resources in an all-stock deal worth about $12.8 billion, creating the largest natural gas producer in the United States. Fortune puts the premium at nearly 21%. The companies expect $325 million a year in savings, and the deal should close in early 2027.

Verizon added 406,000 phone customers on monthly plans, its best third quarter in five years, and raised its outlook for the third quarter in a row. But revenue missed forecasts, and the shares fell.

In insurance, The Hartford's catastrophe losses rose to $198 million from $70 million a year ago, from tornadoes, wind and hail. Cincinnati Financial's combined ratio worsened to 90.9% from 88.2%. Nucor earned $5.80 a share, above its own guidance.

## The world

Saudi Arabia's defense minister, Pakistan's army chief Asim Munir and Turkey's intelligence chief met President Pezeshkian and other Iranian officials in Tehran on Monday. They agreed to keep talking. Iran says it would reopen the Strait of Hormuz if the United States lifts its naval blockade. President Trump says it is getting to be decision time on whether to make a deal with Iran or resume strikes.

In Brazil, President Lula won the runoff with about 52% of the vote. Investors had positioned for his opponent, Flavio Bolsonaro. Brazil's main stock index fell about 15%, trading was halted once, and the real lost more than 10% against the dollar. Bolsonaro has not conceded.

The Kremlin says preparations have begun for a meeting of Presidents Putin, Trump and Xi at the Asia-Pacific summit in Shenzhen next month. Washington and Beijing have not confirmed it.

## Coming up

Today, Coca-Cola, Boeing and UPS report before the New York open, and Visa after the close. The Conference Board publishes consumer confidence. On Wednesday, the Federal Reserve and the Bank of Canada decide, Britain presents its budget, and Microsoft and Alphabet report. Thursday brings gross domestic product, inflation, the European Central Bank, Amazon and Intel. The Bank of Japan decides Friday. That is the briefing. Have a good day.
