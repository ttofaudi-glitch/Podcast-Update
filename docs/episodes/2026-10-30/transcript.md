# Omaha Morning Brief

_Friday, October 30, 2026_

## The short version

Good morning, Warren. It is Friday, October 30th. The American economy grew faster than expected last quarter, but inflation rose again. Alphabet had its worst day as a public company, and Amazon and Intel fell after hours. The European Central Bank held rates, and its president, Christine Lagarde, will leave early.

## Rates and inflation

Gross domestic product grew at a 3.4% annual rate in the third quarter, above the 3.2% expected and up from 2.2%. Business investment rose almost 13%, the fastest since 2020, led by equipment for artificial intelligence. Consumer spending grew 3.8%.

But inflation is rising. The Federal Reserve's preferred measure rose 3.6% from a year earlier in September, up from 3.4%. Without food and energy, it rose 3.1%. Households saved just 3.8% of income, the lowest since 2022. First-time jobless claims stayed low at 194,000.

The 10-year Treasury yield fell to 4.98%, and three-month bills pay 4.18%. Futures put the odds of a Federal Reserve rate increase in December at about four in five.

## Markets

The S&P 500 fell 0.3% to 7,720, and the Nasdaq fell 1.4%. Smaller company stocks rose. Alphabet fell about 13%, its worst day ever, losing close to $500 billion of market value after its earnings miss and $9 billion of negative free cash flow. Brent crude ended slightly lower, near $115, after Vice President Vance sent a letter to an Iranian official through Qatar.

## Companies

Amazon's sales rose 12% to $202 billion. Its cloud business grew 39%, the fastest in nearly five years, and its operating margin hit a record. But earnings missed forecasts, its fourth-quarter sales outlook was below estimates, and free cash flow over the past year was an outflow of $42 billion. It still plans about $220 billion of capital spending this year, and more next year. The shares fell about 7% after hours.

Intel's sales rose 21%, and its data center and artificial intelligence business grew 78%. But it forecast fourth-quarter revenue of $15 billion to $16 billion, against about $17 billion expected, and said the slowdown in personal computers will last at least through the first half of next year. The shares fell more than 16% after hours.

Eli Lilly's revenue rose 39% to $24.4 billion, driven by Mounjaro and Zepbound. Prices fell, but volumes rose much faster. It raised its full-year sales outlook to $91 billion to $92 billion, and the stock rose.

Caterpillar's sales rose 17%, its backlog hit a record $78 billion, and earnings beat forecasts. But it left its annual sales outlook unchanged, and the stock fell about 9%. Mastercard and Merck beat forecasts and rose.

After the close, Starbucks reported global comparable sales up 7.2% and said it will resume share buybacks. In Seoul, Samsung reported a record quarterly operating profit of about 107 trillion won, almost all of it from memory chips.

Aon estimates global insured natural catastrophe losses of $91 billion through September, the lowest for that period since 2019.

## The world

The European Central Bank kept its deposit rate at 2.5% after debating an increase. Christine Lagarde said she will step down on February 7th, about nine months before her term ends.

Vice President Vance wrote to Iran's security chief to try to revive talks. President Trump called it humanitarian outreach and said he did not direct a restart of negotiations. Iran says it has not yet replied.

In Israel, the count shows neither Prime Minister Netanyahu's bloc nor Gadi Eisenkot's with a majority. President Herzog's consultations on who forms a government come next week.

A correction: on Thursday we said Britain's budget passed. It was presented on Wednesday; it has not been voted through.

## Next week

Apple reports Monday after the close. Tuesday is the American midterm election, and Australia's central bank is expected to raise rates. AMD reports Tuesday. On Thursday, the Bank of England is expected to raise its rate to 4%. On Friday, the United States publishes the October jobs report. The Bank of Japan decides later today. That is the briefing. Have a good day.
