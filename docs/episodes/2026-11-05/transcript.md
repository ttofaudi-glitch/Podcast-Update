# Omaha Morning Brief

_Thursday, November 5, 2026_

## The short version

Good morning, Warren. It is Thursday, November 5th. The Associated Press has called both the House and the Senate for the Democrats. The Dow rose 3%, its best day since April 2025. Oil fell about 8%, as traders bet the result pushes Washington toward ending the war with Iran. The Bank of England decides on rates today.

## Rates and inflation

The 10-year Treasury yield fell to 4.90%, and the 2-year to 4.58%. The 3-month bill held at 4.21%.

Private employers added 86,000 jobs in October, according to ADP, more than the roughly 75,000 expected. The Institute for Supply Management's services index slipped to 54.5, a three-month low. Its prices-paid gauge was 73, still high, and companies said they have not yet passed on all of their tariff and fuel costs. Futures markets now put the odds of a Federal Reserve rate increase in December at about four in five.

The Treasury kept its debt sales unchanged at $125 billion this quarter. But it dropped the words at least from its promise to keep auction sizes steady, which leaves room for larger sales next year.

## Markets

The Dow rose 1,569 points, or 3.03%, to 53,356. The S&P 500 rose 1.58%, and the Nasdaq 1.44%. Consumer stocks led, helped by lower gasoline prices. Energy was the weakest group.

Brent crude settled at $94.62 a barrel, down 8.7% and its lowest since August 31st. West Texas Intermediate fell more than 8%, to about $88. American crude inventories fell when a rise had been expected. Gold rose about 3%, back above $4,000 an ounce, and the dollar fell to a five-week low.

## Companies

McDonald's earned $3.57 a share, above estimates, but sales in America at restaurants open at least a year fell 0.2%, as fewer customers came in. It raised its dividend 4%. CVS raised its profit forecast, plans to split into two public companies, and will resume buybacks this quarter. McKesson raised its profit forecast and has returned $3.6 billion to shareholders this year, mostly through buybacks.

After the close, AppLovin rose about 16% on sales growth of 55% and a $3.2 billion addition to its buyback. Qualcomm fell after its profit forecast came in below estimates, as handset chip sales dropped 26%. Arm's record revenue beat estimates, but its shares slipped after hours.

In insurance, Allstate's third-quarter catastrophe losses were $1.65 billion, about three times a year earlier, yet adjusted earnings of $9.76 a share beat estimates by a wide margin. AIG's catastrophe charges nearly tripled, mostly from storms in North America.

## The world

Democrats are on track for at least 54 Senate seats, up from 47. NBC projects their House majority will be the largest in nearly two decades. They won in Texas, North Carolina and Iowa, among others. In Texas, James Talarico beat Ken Paxton, the first Democratic statewide win there since 1994. Races in Alaska and Mississippi are still too close to call. Vice President Vance blamed the losses on the war with Iran. Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer said he would use Congress's spending power to end it. President Trump said he accepts the results.

Iran's chief negotiator said American voters had said no to war. No new American strikes were reported after the vote. Talks through Qatar continue, with no agreement.

In Israel, President Herzog accepted the certified results and began talks with the parties. No bloc has a majority. Russia launched one of its largest attacks on Ukraine's power grid, ahead of three-way talks in Abu Dhabi next week.

## Coming up

The Bank of England announces at noon London time, 8 pm in Hong Kong, and is expected to raise its rate to 4% for the first time since 2023. ConocoPhillips reports today before the open, and Airbnb and Cloudflare after the close. On Friday, the October jobs report is expected to show about 60,000 new jobs and unemployment of 4.2%. China's pause on its rare earth export controls expires on November 10th. That is the briefing. Have a good day.
