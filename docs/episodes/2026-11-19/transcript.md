# Omaha Morning Brief

_Thursday, November 19, 2026_

## The short version

Good morning, Warren. It is Thursday, November 19th. Minutes of the Federal Reserve's October meeting show almost all officials expect more rate increases. The Dow fell to a five-month low. Nvidia fell almost 3% despite its strong results. Anthropic, which makes the system that writes this briefing, priced its share sale above the planned range. And today in Shenzhen, Presidents Xi, Trump and Putin meet.

## Rates and inflation

The minutes show the October hold was a close call. Two officials voted for an increase, and a couple said a half-point move could be considered in December if inflation data are unfavorable. Governor Christopher Waller said on Wednesday the Federal Reserve should raise rates in December and consider half a point. Futures markets treat a December increase as near-certain.

The 10-year Treasury yield eased to 5.07%, and the 2-year held at 4.80%. The 3-month bill pays 4.34%.

Housing starts rose about 10% in October, but building permits fell 7.4%, to their lowest since May 2020. Britain's inflation rose to 3.6%, as expected. Bank Indonesia surprised most economists by raising its rate to 6% to support the rupiah.

## Markets

The Dow fell 561 points, or 1.11%, to 50,198, its lowest close in five months. The S&P 500 fell 0.23%, to 7,324, its fifth straight loss. The Nasdaq rose 0.42%.

Brent crude fell about 1.3%, to near $92 a barrel, after a larger than expected rise in American crude inventories. West Texas Intermediate settled near $84. Gold fell to about $3,750 an ounce, its lowest in more than a year, about 31% below January's record. The dollar is near a 19-month high.

## Companies

Nvidia opened higher but closed down 2.9%, its fifth straight loss. Several analysts cut their price targets, citing uncertainty over who will lead the company. The board says it does not intend to bring back Jensen Huang.

Anthropic priced its shares at $83, above the $70 to $78 range, according to people familiar with the deal. The public offering raises at least about $68 billion, at a reported valuation well over one trillion dollars. Trading is expected to begin today on the Nasdaq.

Target's comparable sales rose 5.6%, its best growth in five years, and it raised its forecast; the shares rose about 7%. Part of its profit came from a refund of tariffs. TJX rose about 10% on higher sales and a raised outlook. Lowe's beat estimates and raised its sales forecast. After the close, Palo Alto Networks beat estimates, raised its outlook and agreed to buy Applied Compute for $4.5 billion in stock. Its shares rose about 7% after hours.

## The world

President Trump arrived in Shenzhen on Wednesday, where President Xi met him at the airport. At the evening gala, he and President Putin shook hands and posed with President Xi. Today the three leaders hold their first three-way meeting, after President Trump's talks with President Xi. China says they will discuss world peace and strategic stability. American officials do not expect agreements to be announced.

In the Gulf, America and Iran have continued to trade strikes, and more ships have hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz. Talks remain stalled.

In Israel, former prime minister Naftali Bennett met Netanyahu for more than two hours, surprising his allies and unsettling Gadi Eisenkot's coalition talks. At the climate talks in Turkey, the first draft deal calls for faster action to phase out fossil fuels, and large producers are pushing back.

A correction. Yesterday we said the Alaska Senate race was going to a ranked-choice count. In fact, the Associated Press called it on Tuesday for Democrat Mary Peltola, with just over 50% of the vote, so no second count is needed. Senator Dan Sullivan has not conceded.

## Coming up

Walmart reports this morning, New York time, expected to earn about 64 cents a share. Weekly jobless claims and the Philadelphia factory survey also come out. Intuit and Ross Stores report after the close. Final November consumer sentiment comes out Friday. Next week, American markets close on Thursday for Thanksgiving. That is the briefing. Have a good day.
