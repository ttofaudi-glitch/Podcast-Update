# Omaha Morning Brief

_Friday, November 13, 2026_

## The short version

Good morning, Warren. It is Friday, November 13th. Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's chief executive, and finance chief Colette Kress is running the company for now. The 10-year Treasury yield rose above 5%. Stocks slipped. Oil rose about 3% after attacks on Saudi energy sites and President Trump's statement that America does not need a deal with Iran. Cisco raised its outlook.

## Rates and inflation

Treasury yields jumped after Wednesday's holiday. The 10-year yield rose to 5.10%, the 2-year to 4.86%, and the 3-month bill to 4.32%. A $25 billion sale of 30-year bonds drew weak demand for the second day in a row, at 5.44%.

Mary Daly, president of the San Francisco Federal Reserve Bank, said her bias is that further increases will be needed, as price pressures spread into services. Futures markets treat a December increase as near-certain.

Weekly jobless claims rose to 200,000, still low. Britain's economy grew 0.7% in the third quarter, its fastest in about two years. American producer prices come out tonight, Hong Kong time.

## Markets

The S&P 500 fell 0.35%, to 7,592. The Nasdaq fell 0.39%, and the Dow 0.36%. AMD and Broadcom rose more than 5%.

Brent crude rose about 3%, to near $96 a barrel, and West Texas Intermediate to about $89. Yemen's Houthis said they attacked a Saudi pipeline pumping station and an oil facility at Yanbu; Saudi Arabia said it shot down two drones. Iran said four tankers were hit in the Strait of Hormuz, which is not confirmed. Gold fell about 3%, to a one-year low near $3,870. The yen weakened toward 160 to the dollar, and Japan warned again against sharp moves.

## Companies

Jensen Huang, who co-founded Nvidia, resigned as chief executive and left the board after reports about his personal conduct, including an alleged link to a foreign intelligence effort. The board named its first independent chair and will search inside and outside the company for a successor. The shares fell as much as 14% before closing down about 4%. Nvidia reports results on Tuesday. Super Micro Computer fell 12.7% after Bloomberg reported that the Justice Department is examining whether its hardware was diverted to China.

After the close, Cisco's revenue rose 24%, to $18.5 billion, and it raised its full-year forecast. It now expects $9 billion of artificial intelligence orders from the largest cloud companies this year. It returned $3.7 billion to shareholders in the quarter. Applied Materials reported record revenue, up 53%, and forecast higher sales next quarter. Nebius, an artificial intelligence cloud company, fell about 16% after saying capacity delays will hold this year's revenue to the low end of its forecast.

In insurance, Allianz's natural catastrophe losses were about €1.1 billion in the quarter, mostly hail in Europe, against almost nothing a year earlier. It still raised its profit outlook. Munich Re confirmed a higher full-year profit target of €6.6 billion, as large losses came in below budget.

## The world

American envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met President Putin in Moscow for about two hours. Witkoff said a new round of talks has been set, with no date. The Kremlin called the talks useful, and Putin again rejected foreign peacekeepers in Ukraine. The envoys then met President Zelensky near Dublin.

President Trump wrote that America does not need a deal with Iran. Iran says the Strait of Hormuz stays closed until the blockade and sanctions are lifted. He also threatened to double tariffs on Mexico over migrant caravans.

An update on yesterday: Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith conceded the Mississippi Senate race to Democrat Scott Colom. Alaska is still counting.

## Next week

Tuesday is busy: American retail sales, results from Home Depot and Disney in the morning, and Nvidia after the close, its first report without Jensen Huang. On Wednesday, Federal Reserve minutes, British inflation, and results from Target, Lowe's and TJX. Leaders meet in Shenzhen for the Asia-Pacific summit on Wednesday and Thursday, where President Trump expects to see President Xi. Walmart reports on Thursday. Government funding runs out on December 11th. That is the briefing. Have a good day.
