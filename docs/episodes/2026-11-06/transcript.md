# Omaha Morning Brief

_Friday, November 6, 2026_

## The short version

Good morning, Warren. It is Friday, November 6th. Stocks gave back Wednesday's election rally and more. The S&P 500 fell 4.36%, and the Nasdaq entered a correction, as investors sold chip and artificial intelligence stocks on fears that a Democratic Congress will slow the building of data centers. The Bank of England raised its rate to 4%. Oil rose again after more attacks on tankers.

## Rates and inflation

Investors moved into Treasury bonds. The 10-year yield fell to 4.84%, and the 2-year fell sharply, to 4.46%. The 3-month bill was 4.17%.

Weekly claims for unemployment benefits stayed below 200,000, near their lowest since the late 1960s. Productivity rose at an annual rate of 3.6% in the third quarter, and labor costs per unit of output fell 0.7%. That is good news for margins and for inflation. Still, futures markets put the odds of a Federal Reserve rate increase in December at about four in five.

The Bank of England raised its rate to 4%, its first increase since 2023. The vote was eight to one; one member wanted a larger rise. The Bank expects British inflation to reach about 5% by mid-2027 because of energy prices, and warned that more increases are likely if the war with Iran continues. Sweden's central bank also raised its rate this week, while Norway's held at 4.5%.

## Markets

The Dow fell 1,833 points, or 3.44%, to 51,523, erasing Wednesday's gain. The S&P 500 closed at 7,354. The Nasdaq fell 5.52% and is now more than 10% below its October record. All eleven sectors of the S&P 500 fell. The Philadelphia chip index fell more than 12%, and Nvidia and Broadcom fell between 7% and 10%.

Brent crude rose 3.9%, to $98.29 a barrel, and West Texas Intermediate to $90.52. Gold is near $4,100 an ounce, up about 4% this week.

## Companies

ConocoPhillips earned $3.27 a share, about double a year ago, on higher oil prices. It raised its dividend 11% and bought back $2.4 billion of stock in the quarter. Cigna raised its profit forecast.

AppLovin rose 31%, the best gain in the S&P 500, after its strong results. Qualcomm fell about 10% on its weak forecast. CVS fell more than 12% on Wednesday after announcing its split.

After the close, Airbnb's revenue rose 19%, to about $4.9 billion, above estimates, and it raised its full-year forecast. Its shares rose after hours. Cloudflare's revenue rose 39%, and its shares jumped about 17% after a 25% fall over the past week. Atlassian and DraftKings fell sharply.

## The world

Iran said more tankers struck mines in the Strait of Hormuz, and its Revolutionary Guard called the strait closed to ships that do not coordinate with it. Those claims are not independently confirmed. President Trump said he has no immediate plans for new strikes. Eurasia Group, a political risk firm, cut its odds of a deal to one in ten.

President Trump said Democrats are not really in control, because he can veto their bills. Democrats plan early votes to end the Iran war and to undo his tariffs. The Senate races in Alaska and Mississippi are still too close to call.

France's National Assembly rejected next year's budget by 458 votes to none. The government's original bill now goes to the Senate. A clarification: yesterday we said China's pause on rare earth export controls expires on November 10th. Washington says the wider trade truce, which runs to January 10th, covers it. Beijing has not said so.

## Next week

Tonight, Hong Kong time, the October jobs report is expected to show about 60,000 new jobs and unemployment of 4.2%. On Sunday, Israel's President Herzog decides who will try to form a government. Tuesday brings American consumer prices and China's October trade figures. Russia, Ukraine and the United States meet in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday and Wednesday. Britain reports third-quarter growth on Thursday, and Cisco reports that evening. Producer prices follow on Friday. That is the briefing. Have a good day.
