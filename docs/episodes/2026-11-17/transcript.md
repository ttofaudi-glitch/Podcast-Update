# Omaha Morning Brief

_Tuesday, November 17, 2026_

## The short version

Good morning, Warren. It is Tuesday, November 17th. Stocks fell sharply, led by chip makers, after the Commerce Department moved to stop Chinese companies renting American artificial intelligence chips in other countries. Nvidia closed at its lowest in more than a year, a day before its results. Japan's economy shrank in the third quarter, and China's October figures all missed forecasts.

## Rates and inflation

The 10-year Treasury yield eased to 5.12%, and the 2-year to 4.83%. The 3-month bill pays 4.34%.

Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh spoke about artificial intelligence on Monday. He warned of risks, including job losses or a steep fall in stock prices if the expected returns do not arrive, but said nothing about interest rates. Futures markets put the odds of a December increase at about nine in ten.

Japan's economy shrank at an annual rate of 0.1% in the third quarter, its first fall in a year, as consumers cut back. In China, October factory output rose 4.8% and retail sales only 0.4%, both below forecasts, and investment fell 8.1% so far this year.

## Markets

The S&P 500 fell 1.92%, to 7,417, its third loss in a row. The Nasdaq fell 2.49%, and the Dow 739 points, or 1.41%. All eleven sectors fell, led by technology. The Philadelphia chip index fell more than 5%, with Micron down about 7% and AMD about 5.5%.

Oil prices fell on signs of ample supply. Gold fell below $3,900 an ounce, its lowest in more than a year, as markets bet on higher American rates. The dollar stayed near 160 yen.

## Companies

Nvidia fell 4.1%, to about $156, its lowest close in more than a year and about 35% below its October peak. The new Commerce rule requires licenses for advanced chips sent to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil, and for any data center that serves companies based in China. Nvidia reports tonight, after the New York close. The company guided to revenue of about $108 billion, and analysts expect earnings of about $2.48 a share, against $1.30 a year ago; investors will listen closely to interim chief executive Colette Kress. Jensen Huang has agreed to testify to House committees; no date is set.

Diamondback Energy agreed to buy oil assets in the Midland Basin from Double Eagle for $2.15 billion in cash plus about 15.6 million of its shares. Its stock fell about 3%. Tyson Foods rose about 8% on its results.

Home Depot and Disney report this morning, New York time. Disney's quarter includes an extra week.

## The world

President Trump arrives in Manila today, then goes to Shenzhen. The White House and the Kremlin say he will meet Presidents Xi and Putin together on Thursday. Beijing has not confirmed it. A senior American official said no agreements are expected to be announced.

A third American aircraft carrier has arrived in the Middle East as talks with Iran have stalled. Iran says more tankers hit mines over the weekend, which is not confirmed, and Gulf states condemned an Iranian attack on Bahrain.

Russia stepped up strikes on Ukraine's power grid as winter begins. Ukraine says it struck the bridge to Crimea, which was closed.

In Israel, unity talks between Gadi Eisenkot's party and Likud made no progress; both men want to be prime minister.

In Alaska, Democrat Mary Peltola leads with almost all ballots counted, but just under 50%, so the Senate race likely goes to a ranked-choice count. At the COP31 climate talks in Turkey, a first draft of the final deal was released, with finance and fossil fuels still unresolved.

## Coming up

American retail sales for October come out tonight, Hong Kong time, expected to rise about 0.2%. Nvidia reports after the close. On Wednesday, the Federal Reserve releases its October minutes, and Target, Lowe's and TJX report. Anthropic, which makes the system that writes this briefing, is expected to price its share sale on Wednesday and begin trading Thursday. Walmart reports Thursday, the same day as the Shenzhen meeting. That is the briefing. Have a good day.
