# Omaha Morning Brief

_Thursday, October 22, 2026_

## The short version

Good morning, Warren. It is Thursday, October 22nd. The S&P 500 rose for a third day and is close to its record again. Meta's sales grew 26%, but its spending on artificial intelligence now exceeds the cash its business produces. Tesla's margins recovered. And inflation and government debt are pressing on Britain and France.

## Rates and inflation

The 10-year Treasury yield ended at 5.13%. The Treasury sold $13 billion of 20-year bonds at a yield of 5.469%, with demand above average. Three-month Treasury bills pay 4.25%. Futures put the odds of a Federal Reserve rate increase next Wednesday at about one in six, and see one by December as very likely. The average 30-year mortgage rate eased to 7.41% from 7.50%, its highest since late 2023, and mortgage applications to buy homes fell 7% in a week.

In Britain, consumer prices rose 3.5% from a year earlier in September, above the 3.4% expected and up from 3.1%, driven by fuel. Markets now expect the Bank of England to raise its rate on November 5.

## Markets

The S&P 500 rose 0.6% to 7,757. The Dow rose 0.7% and the Nasdaq 1.1%. For the week so far, the S&P is up 1.3%. Netflix fell another 5%, to its lowest close since August 2024, after cutting its advertising forecast. Brent crude stayed near $109. United States crude stocks fell by 3.1 million barrels last week, more than expected.

## Companies

Meta's revenue rose 26% to $64.6 billion, ahead of forecasts, and it guided to $73 billion to $76 billion for the fourth quarter. But capital spending was $34.9 billion in the quarter, and free cash flow was negative, about $2.1 billion. Profit included a $9.9 billion charge for its settlement with state attorneys general over teenagers' mental health. Separately, a New Mexico judge ordered Meta to pay $25 billion in a privacy case; Meta is expected to appeal. Meta also plans a new class of non-voting shares. The stock rose after hours.

Tesla's revenue was flat at $28 billion. Its gross margin rose to 19.6% from 16.8% in the second quarter, and free cash flow was $1.5 billion after a negative second quarter. Operating income still fell 56%, as spending on artificial intelligence and its Optimus robot grew. Separately, Tesla and SpaceX have each formed board committees to study a merger. Elon Musk said a deal is not certain.

IBM's revenue rose 5% to $17.1 billion, ahead of forecasts, with software up 8%. It raised its outlook for free cash flow.

AT&T beat forecasts, raised its profit outlook and plans about $10 billion of buybacks this year. But the stock fell about 5% on worries that SpaceX's planned Starlink mobile service will add competition.

In insurance, Chubb's catastrophe losses rose to $705 million from $285 million a year ago, from weather and a Latin American earthquake. Its combined ratio rose to 84.1% from 81.8%. Excluding catastrophes, it was about flat.

## The world

France's 10-year government bond yield briefly topped 5% on Wednesday, for the first time since 2002, before easing just below it. The gap over German yields is the widest in about 15 years. Moody's is due to review France's credit rating on Friday.

Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, said the Strait of Hormuz stays closed until the United States accepts Iran's conditions, starting with an end to the naval blockade. He said talks continue through Pakistan and Qatar. The White House says the blockade remains in full force.

Japan's September exports rose 16.7% from a year earlier, helped by demand for artificial intelligence chips.

## Coming up

Today, Procter & Gamble, Lockheed Martin, Blackstone and Union Pacific report, along with weekly jobless claims. On Friday, American Express reports, Japan publishes consumer prices, the University of Michigan releases its final October survey, and Moody's reviews France. Next week brings the British budget and the Federal Reserve decision on Wednesday, and the European Central Bank on Thursday. That is the briefing. Have a good day.
