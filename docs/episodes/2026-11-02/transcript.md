# Omaha Morning Brief

_Monday, November 2, 2026_

## The short version

Good morning, Warren. It is Monday, November 2nd. Technology stocks ended October badly. On Friday the S&P 500 had its worst day since June, and Intel had its worst day since 1974. Bond yields fell as investors looked for safety. Oil companies reported record refining profits. And this week brings the American midterm election, the Bank of England and the October jobs report.

## Rates and inflation

The 10-year Treasury yield ended the week at 4.94%, down from 5.29% at the end of September. Three-month Treasury bills pay 4.16%.

Two Federal Reserve officials pushed for more increases. Alberto Musalem, president of the St. Louis Federal Reserve Bank, said more tightening is needed soon, and Lorie Logan of the Dallas Federal Reserve Bank called for at least two more quarter-point increases. Futures put the odds of a December increase at about seven in ten, down from about four in five a few days earlier.

Employers' labor costs rose 3.4% from a year earlier in the third quarter. In Europe, the economy grew 0.4% in the third quarter, while France's shrank slightly.

## Markets

On Friday the S&P 500 fell 1.8% to 7,580, and the Nasdaq fell 2.9%. The Dow fell 1.1%. The S&P lost about 0.6% for the week, its third weekly loss in a row, and about 0.9% for October. The Nasdaq lost more than 5% in October, its worst month since March 2025.

Brent crude ended the week near $113, up more than 10% in October. Gold fell below $4,000 an ounce, its lowest in about a year, as investors priced in higher interest rates.

## Companies

Intel fell nearly 29% after forecasting fourth-quarter revenue well below estimates, citing weak personal computer demand and an explosion at its factory in Israel. Other chip makers fell, including AMD by about 11%. Amazon recovered most of its early losses and ended down about 1.4%.

Exxon Mobil earned $17.5 billion on an adjusted basis, and its refining business earned more than its oil and gas production. It returned $9.4 billion to shareholders in the quarter, raised its dividend, and plans $20 billion of buybacks next year. Chevron's refining profit rose to $4.9 billion from $1.1 billion a year ago, its American refineries ran a record amount of crude, and it cut net debt by $7.8 billion. Both stocks closed at records. Chevron's finance chief expects margins to stay high for now and ease later.

AbbVie raised its outlook and its dividend.

In deals, DoorDash agreed to buy Instacart for $65 a share, about $17 billion, mostly in cash. It faces antitrust review and is not expected to close until late 2027 or 2028.

## The world

Iranian negotiators held a third day of talks with Qatari mediators in Doha on Sunday. Foreign Minister Araghchi said a plan would reopen the Strait of Hormuz within two days of the United States meeting Iran's conditions, including lifting the blockade and releasing frozen funds. He said there is no breakthrough yet. Secretary of State Rubio said President Trump has not decided on new strikes and prefers diplomacy.

OPEC and its partners kept December oil output targets unchanged on Sunday. The bigger meeting, which will set quotas for 2027, is on November 29.

China's official factory index fell to 49.3 in October, back below 50, which marks contraction. The Bank of Japan held its rate at 1.25%, with one member voting for an increase. It raised its inflation forecast and said underlying inflation is now close to its 2% goal, which keeps a December increase in play.

In Israel, the final count gives Prime Minister Netanyahu's bloc 53 seats and Gadi Eisenkot's 48. President Herzog begins consultations on Wednesday.

## Coming up

Apple reports today after the New York close, the first report under its new chief executive, John Ternus. Tuesday is election day, and Australia's central bank is expected to raise rates. AMD reports Tuesday, and McDonald's on Wednesday. Thursday, the Bank of England is expected to raise its rate to 4%. On Friday, the October jobs report is expected to show about 60,000 new jobs. That is the briefing. Have a good day.
