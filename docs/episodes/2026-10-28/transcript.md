# Omaha Morning Brief

_Wednesday, October 28, 2026_

## The short version

Good morning, Warren. It is Wednesday, October 28th. The Federal Reserve decides on rates today, with a hold widely expected. American consumers turned more confident as gasoline prices eased. Boeing fell sharply after pushing back its production plans. And Meta sold $25 billion of stock, part of a plan to raise nearly $100 billion.

## Rates and inflation

The 10-year Treasury yield ended at 4.99%, and three-month Treasury bills pay 4.22%. The Treasury sold $70 billion of five-year notes at 4.845%, with average demand. Futures put the odds of a rate increase today at about one in ten, and about three in four for one in December. The decision comes at 2 a.m. Thursday, Hong Kong time.

The Conference Board's consumer confidence index jumped to 91.7 in October from 84.2, far above the 81 expected, the highest since June. The board credited easing gasoline prices. Consumers still expect inflation of close to 6% over the next year. Home prices rose 2.1% from a year earlier in August, which is less than inflation.

## Markets

The S&P 500 rose 0.6% to 7,723, and the Nasdaq rose 1.2%. The Dow was flat, held back by Boeing. Chip stocks rose for a second day.

Brent crude settled at $113.88, down 1.7%, as talks in Tehran continued. West Texas Intermediate fell back below $100.

## Companies

Boeing's shares fell about 10%, their worst day since 2020. It lost 34 cents a share on a core basis, more than expected, though free cash flow was positive at $464 million. Steady production of 47 of its 737 jets a month has slipped into 2027, and its target of $10 billion a year of free cash flow now points to 2029. Net debt fell to $25.2 billion from $30.4 billion a year ago.

Coca-Cola's organic revenue rose 7%, with unit case volume up 5%. It raised its full-year outlook for the third quarter in a row, now expecting comparable earnings per share to grow at least 11%. North American volume was flat, while Latin America grew strongly.

Meta priced $25 billion of common stock at $625 a share, about 3% below Friday's close, and the sale drew about five times as many orders as shares on offer. It also sold $12.5 billion of preferred stock that will convert into common shares. Nvidia will invest $3 billion and AMD $1 billion.

UPS raised its full-year outlook on Tuesday morning. After the close, Visa's revenue rose 13% to $12.1 billion and cross-border volume grew 14%. It expects slower earnings growth next year, and the shares fell about 2% after hours.

## The world

The Houthis said they hit Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery and export terminal with a missile. Saudi Arabia says it intercepted missiles, and a fire near the terminal was contained. Damage is not confirmed. Iran's leaders say the war ends only when their conditions are met, starting with an end to the American blockade.

Israel held an election on Tuesday. Exit polls show neither Prime Minister Netanyahu's bloc nor Gadi Eisenkot's opposition bloc with a majority, so coalition talks could take weeks.

China's industrial profits rose 14.4% from a year earlier in September, led by electronics tied to artificial intelligence and by mining. Other industries are squeezed by weak demand at home.

A correction: on Tuesday we said Brazil's stock index fell about 15% after President Lula's re-election. It fell more than 15% during the day but closed down 13.9%. On Tuesday it slipped another half percent, and the real firmed.

Britain presents its budget today, with markets watching whether it rebuilds room under its borrowing rules.

## Coming up

Today, the Federal Reserve and the Bank of Canada decide, Britain presents its budget, and Microsoft and Alphabet report after the close. Thursday brings third-quarter gross domestic product, September inflation, the European Central Bank, and Amazon, Intel, Caterpillar and Mastercard. The Bank of Japan decides on Friday, and Exxon and Chevron report. That is the briefing. Have a good day.
