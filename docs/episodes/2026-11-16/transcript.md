# Omaha Morning Brief

_Monday, November 16, 2026_

## The short version

Good morning, Warren. It is Monday, November 16th. Stocks ended last week higher, but the 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 5.15%, up about three-tenths of a point in a week. Producer prices are up 6.5% from a year earlier. Nvidia reports on Tuesday, its first report since Jensen Huang resigned. And the White House and the Kremlin say Presidents Trump, Xi and Putin will meet in China on Thursday.

## Rates and inflation

The 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.15% on Friday, up about three-tenths of a point in a week. The 2-year ended at 4.88%, and the 3-month bill at 4.32%. Demand at last week's 3-, 10- and 30-year auctions was weak.

Producer prices rose 0.3% in October, a little less than expected, but 6.5% from a year earlier, the most since 2022. Food prices rose and diesel fell.

Futures markets put the odds of a quarter-point Federal Reserve increase in December at close to nine in ten, and of half a point at about one in nine. Chair Kevin Warsh speaks today. The minutes of the October meeting come out on Wednesday.

## Markets

On Friday the S&P 500 fell 0.4%, to 7,562, and the Nasdaq fell 0.9%. The Dow rose 0.1%. For the week, the S&P 500 rose 2.5%, ending four weeks of losses. Cisco fell about 6% despite beating estimates, as investors focused on narrower margins. AMD fell almost 9%.

Brent crude settled at $95.84 a barrel, and West Texas Intermediate at $88.56, a second weekly fall. The International Energy Agency said the oil market will be in surplus this quarter for the first time since the war began. Gold had its worst week in four months, near $3,880 an ounce. The dollar ended near 160 yen, and Prime Minister Takaichi warned again about intervention.

## Companies

Nvidia fell about 2% on Friday, its sixth straight weekly decline. The board says it has not yet formally approached any candidates for chief executive. The Financial Times reported that it had approached Arm's chief executive, Rene Haas; neither company commented. Jensen Huang has agreed to answer questions from House committees behind closed doors before Thanksgiving. Analysts expect revenue of about $109 billion on Tuesday.

Japan's three largest banks all raised their full-year profit targets after higher interest rates lifted earnings. Mitsubishi UFJ and Sumitomo Mitsui together announced about 420 billion yen of buybacks, and Mizuho raised its dividend. Richemont, the owner of Cartier, said first-half sales rose 16%, led by jewelry.

Diamondback Energy is reported to be near a $5 billion deal for Permian Basin oil assets, according to the Financial Times. Modine rose about 29% after Bloomberg reported that Samsung is in early talks to buy it.

## The world

The White House said Presidents Trump, Xi and Putin will hold their first three-way meeting on Thursday, at the Asia-Pacific summit in Shenzhen, with nuclear arms reduction on the agenda. The Kremlin confirmed it; Beijing has not yet.

Iran says more tankers hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz over the weekend; that is not confirmed, and the American military says the main routes are clear. Iran also claimed a drone attack on an American radar in Bahrain; the United States says it was not damaged. The United Nations says at least 25 seafarers have died around the strait since February.

Russia accepted what Ukraine calls the largest prisoner exchange of the war, with no date set. European leaders named Britain, France, Germany, Italy and Poland to represent Europe in the talks. In Alaska, Democrat Mary Peltola has widened her lead in the uncalled Senate race.

## Coming up

Japan reports third-quarter growth this morning, expected to be close to flat, and China reports October retail sales and factory output at 10 o'clock Hong Kong time. Tuesday brings American retail sales, Home Depot and Disney, and Nvidia after the close. Anthropic, which makes the system that writes this briefing, is expected to price its stock market listing midweek, at about $1 trillion. Target, Lowe's and TJX report Wednesday, and Walmart on Thursday. That is the briefing. Have a good day.
