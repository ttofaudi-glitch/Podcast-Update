# Omaha Morning Brief

_Tuesday, November 24, 2026_

## The short version

Good morning, Warren. It is Tuesday, November 24th. American stocks recovered much of last week's loss in one day. Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive, and the shares rose about 31%. The S&P 500 gained 4.7%, its best day since April 2025. Oil fell to its lowest level since August.

## Rates and inflation

Treasury yields held steady. The 10-year yield was unchanged at 4.92%, and the 2-year at 4.63%. The 3-month bill rose to 4.37%, so cash now pays within about half a point of the 10-year bond.

Futures markets now give about 96% odds that the Federal Reserve raises its rate by a quarter point at its December 8th and 9th meeting, up from about 92% on Friday. Traders dropped bets on a half-point move.

The Chicago Federal Reserve's national activity index for October slipped to minus 0.06, a sign of growth slightly below trend.

## Markets

The S&P 500 closed at 7,237, up 4.73%. The Nasdaq rose 6.51% to 24,090, and the Dow added 1,448 points, or 3%, to 49,595. The Philadelphia chip index rose more than 12%. Even so, the S&P 500 remains below where it started last week.

In Europe, the Stoxx 600 rose about 1.9%, led by chipmakers. Asia closed before the Nvidia news. Korea's Kospi fell 3.3%, Hong Kong's Hang Seng was little changed, and Japan was closed for a holiday.

Brent crude settled at $89.37 a barrel, down 3.5%, and West Texas Intermediate at $80.17, down 4.5%. Traders pointed to a forecast supply surplus, more Middle East shipments and a restarted Libyan pipeline, despite new attacks on tankers. Gold rose about 2%, to near $3,600 an ounce.

## Companies

Nvidia's board voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, chief executive and director, effective immediately, less than two weeks after he resigned. The board said it had input from senior levels of the United States government and sees no national security risk in his return. President Trump called it the correct decision. Colette Kress returns to her role as chief financial officer.

Two matters remain open. Nvidia's independent investigation of Mr. Huang continues, and Reuters reported on Friday that the Securities and Exchange Commission is examining the company's accounting. Nvidia also has not yet filed its delayed quarterly report. After Monday's gain, the shares are still about 30% below their record.

CrowdStrike agreed to buy Zscaler for about $44 billion in stock, promising $500 million in cost savings. CrowdStrike shares fell about 9%. Amazon agreed to pay a reported $7.5 billion for Decart's chip optimization technology and its founders, to strengthen its own chips against Nvidia.

## The world

Ambassadors of the North Atlantic alliance met in Brussels on Monday after Russian cruise missiles landed in Poland on Friday. Secretary General Mark Rutte said the alliance will defend every inch of Polish territory. Poland asked for more air defense, including surveillance aircraft, but did not formally invoke the treaty's consultation clause.

In the Strait of Hormuz, another tanker was hit by an unknown projectile early Monday, and shipping remains heavily restricted. President Trump warned Iran against trying to run the American blockade. Iran says it has held talks with Qatari mediators but not with the United States.

In Israel, coalition talks between Gadi Eisenkot's party and Likud met again on Monday without significant progress. Eisenkot has about 29 days left to form a government.

## Coming up

Today brings consumer confidence, and results from Medtronic, Best Buy and Analog Devices, then Dell, HP, Workday and Autodesk after the close. Wednesday is the heavy day: revised third-quarter growth, expected near 3.4%, October inflation on the Federal Reserve's preferred measure, with core prices expected up about 3.2% from a year ago, durable goods orders, jobless claims, and results from Deere. American markets close Thursday for Thanksgiving and close early on Friday. The Bank of Korea decides on Thursday, and economists are split between a hold and an increase to 3.25%. European Union leaders meet Thursday and Friday on a 45 billion euro loan for Ukraine. That is the briefing. Have a good day.
