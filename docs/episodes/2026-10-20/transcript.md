# Omaha Morning Brief

_Tuesday, October 20, 2026_

## The short version

Good morning, Warren. It is Tuesday, October 20th. Stocks bounced on Monday, led by the chip makers, but the bond market is still worried about how artificial intelligence is being financed. China's economy grew faster than expected. And American home builders are the gloomiest they have been in 14 years.

## Rates and inflation

The 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.15% on Monday, from 5.09% on Friday. Three-month Treasury bills pay 4.27%. The Federal Reserve is in its quiet period before its October 28 decision. Futures put the odds of a rate increase then at roughly one in six, but see one in December as likely.

Higher rates are hitting housing. The home builders' confidence index fell to 29 in October, the lowest since June 2012. Forty-three percent of builders cut prices, the highest share since at least 2020, and the average cut was 6%. Their trade group says mortgage rates are well above 7%.

## Markets

The S&P 500 rose 0.4% to 7,691, and the Nasdaq rose 1.1%. The Dow was flat. The Philadelphia semiconductor index ended its eight-day losing streak.

The credit market did not follow. Oracle's credit default swaps, the price of insurance against a default, widened to about 315 basis points, more than four times a year ago. Oracle is still rated investment grade. CoreWeave's swaps were near 1,080 basis points, a level that usually signals distress, and one of its bonds yields about 15%. Goldman Sachs upgraded CoreWeave's stock on Monday and called the debt fears overblown, but the shares gave up most of an early 7% jump.

## Companies

Cleveland-Cliffs, the steel maker, beat forecasts and rose sharply. Its operating cash profit more than doubled from the second quarter to $612 million, as selling prices rose $53 a ton. It expects more than $4 billion of that profit in 2027 and plans to cut debt significantly this quarter.

In insurance, W. R. Berkley's combined ratio was 90.8%, with catastrophes adding 2.7 points. Its revenue missed estimates. Florida's insurance regulator counts about 47,000 claims and $582 million of insured losses from Hurricane Isaias so far, with most claims still open.

Among banks, Fifth Third's adjusted earnings beat forecasts, though revenue fell short. Its net charge-offs were a low 0.31% of loans.

In deals, Tradeweb agreed to buy Trumid, an electronic bond trading platform, for about $1.97 billion, mostly in cash on hand. Merck KGaA of Germany agreed to buy Silence Therapeutics for $20 a share in cash, a 130% premium.

## The world

Brent crude settled at $109.55, down 0.3%. It fell more than $3 early on Trump's waiver of Russia sanctions, then recovered after the Houthis said they attacked a Saudi Aramco facility at Yanbu and the Shaybah oilfield. Saudi Arabia has not confirmed the attacks.

Over the weekend, Ukraine launched more than 1,000 drones at Russia and set fire to an oil export terminal in St. Petersburg. President Trump criticized Zelenskyy for the attack and said he will hold off on new sanctions against Russia. Russia's foreign minister, Sergei Lavrov, visited Tehran on Monday and called for the Strait of Hormuz to return to pre-war conditions.

China's economy grew 4.5% from a year earlier in the third quarter, a little above forecasts. Growth for the first nine months was 4.6%, inside Beijing's target of 4.5% to 5%. Retail sales rose 1.1% in September, the fastest since March, but property investment is down more than 20% this year.

In France, violent weekend protests over living costs pushed the 10-year government bond yield up about 10 basis points to near 4.9%, now above Italy's. Prime Minister Lecornu's minority government still has to pass next year's budget.

## Coming up

Today in New York, General Motors reports before the open and Netflix after the close, along with September housing starts. On Wednesday, Tesla, Meta and IBM report, and Britain publishes September inflation. Procter & Gamble reports Thursday, a test of pricing power against higher costs, and American Express reports Friday. That is the briefing. Have a good day.
