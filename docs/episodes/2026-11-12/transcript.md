# Omaha Morning Brief

_Thursday, November 12, 2026_

## The short version

Good morning, Warren. It is Thursday, November 12th. Stocks rose as CoreWeave jumped about 25% on its results and revived chip and artificial intelligence shares. Federal Reserve officials lined up behind a December rate increase. Russia and Ukraine agreed to a large prisoner exchange in Abu Dhabi, but made no progress on territory. And the House shelved the impeachment of Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth.

## Rates and inflation

The bond market was closed on Wednesday for Veterans Day. As of Tuesday, the 10-year Treasury yield was 4.98%, the 2-year 4.73%, and the 3-month bill 4.30%.

After Tuesday's hot inflation report, Governor Christopher Waller said a December increase looks likely and he is open to half a point. Lorie Logan, president of the Dallas Federal Reserve Bank, said rates are at least half a point below where they need to be. Futures markets treat a December increase as near-certain, with about a one in ten chance of half a point.

Today brings weekly jobless claims and a $25 billion sale of 30-year bonds. Producer prices follow on Friday.

## Markets

The S&P 500 rose 1.31%, to 7,619. The Nasdaq rose 1.6%, and the Dow 0.49%. The Philadelphia chip index rose more than 5%. Micron, SanDisk and Coherent all rose more than 10%, and Apple reached a record high.

Brent crude rose about 1.4%, to about $92.76 a barrel, after Iran's security chief denied any talks with Washington and called reports of a meeting in Qatar lies. West Texas Intermediate settled near $86.50. Gold slipped just below $4,000 an ounce, and the dollar rose. The yen weakened to about 159 to the dollar, close to the level where Japan bought yen in July. Japan disclosed this week that it spent a record 15.4 trillion yen on that intervention, about $97 billion.

## Companies

CoreWeave rose about 25%, its biggest one-day gain in about 17 months, after Tuesday's report of 168% revenue growth and a raised forecast. The stock had fallen from about $187 in June 2025 to about $41 last week.

Nvidia rose about 4%, after losing nearly 10% in two days. Baird cut its rating to neutral and its price target to $200 from $500, citing possible cuts in spending by the large cloud companies and uncertainty over the chief executive. House Republicans said they are likely to subpoena Jensen Huang.

In Germany, Siemens Energy reported record annual results and announced a new buyback of up to nine billion euros. Its shares rose about 7%. Corteva cut its full-year outlook because of weak sales in Brazil.

## The world

Russia and Ukraine agreed in principle to a large prisoner exchange after two days of talks in Abu Dhabi, with numbers still to be set. There was no progress on territory. Russia's chief negotiator, Vladimir Medinsky, said a settlement seemed closer, but repeated Russia's claims to Ukrainian land. American envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner go to Moscow to meet President Putin.

Iran says the Strait of Hormuz stays closed until its seven conditions are met. President Trump says the United States has won the war and is holding off on new strikes for now. American officials call Iran's demands outrageous.

The House voted 211 to 204 to send the Hegseth impeachment articles to committee, which effectively ends them in this Congress. Thomas Massie was the only Republican to vote against shelving them. Jim Jordan was elected to lead House Republicans in the next Congress. Government funding runs out on December 11th, and the two parties are far apart. Democrats lead in the Senate races in Mississippi and Alaska, which are still uncalled.

In Israel, Gadi Eisenkot broke off coalition talks with Netanyahu's Likud party. In Turkey, world leaders met at the COP31 climate summit, without the leaders of America, China or India.

## Coming up

Britain reports third-quarter growth this morning, expected at 0.7%. Munich Re, Allianz and Siemens report today. Cisco and Applied Materials report after the New York close. Weekly American crude inventories come out today, a day late because of the holiday. On Friday, producer prices and results from Japan's three largest banks. That is the briefing. Have a good day.
