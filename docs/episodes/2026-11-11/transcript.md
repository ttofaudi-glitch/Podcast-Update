# Omaha Morning Brief

_Wednesday, November 11, 2026_

## The short version

Good morning, Warren. It is Wednesday, November 11th. American consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, more than expected. Treasury yields jumped, and markets now treat a Federal Reserve rate increase in December as close to certain. Stocks slipped, led by another fall in Nvidia. Oil fell again. And a correction on Iran from yesterday's briefing.

## Rates and inflation

Consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, against 0.3% expected. Prices excluding food and energy rose 0.3%, the third month in a row at that pace, against 0.2% expected. Shelter rose 0.3%, food 0.5%, and energy 0.7%. There is no annual rate this month; in September it was 3.7%.

Governor Christopher Waller had said a core reading of 0.3% would put a half-point increase on the table. Futures markets now price a December increase as near-certain, with about a one in seven chance of half a point. Austan Goolsbee of the Chicago Federal Reserve Bank called the report discouraging. Michelle Bowman, the Federal Reserve's vice chair for supervision, said there is still work to do on inflation, and warned of a possible stock correction if artificial intelligence investments disappoint.

The 2-year Treasury yield jumped to 4.73%, and the 10-year to 4.98%. A $42 billion sale of 10-year notes drew weak demand, at 5.02%. The 3-month bill now pays 4.30%. Small business confidence was steady, just below its long-run average.

## Markets

The S&P 500 fell 0.38%, to 7,521. The Nasdaq fell 0.74%, and the Dow 0.30%. Technology was the weakest group. GE Vernova fell about 7% after SoftBank took a $3.7 billion charge on power equipment for American data centers.

Brent crude settled at about $91.50 a barrel, down about 1%, after briefly trading below $90 for the first time since August. West Texas Intermediate settled at $86.04. Gold fell more than 2%, to about $4,000 an ounce, as yields and the dollar rose.

## Companies

Nvidia fell 5.5%, its second straight loss, and has given up its gains for the year. The chairman of the House Foreign Affairs Committee threatened to subpoena chief executive Jensen Huang if he does not agree to a closed-door briefing, as the board's investigation continues.

After the close, CoreWeave's revenue rose 168%, to $3.66 billion, above estimates. Its order backlog grew to about $132 billion, and it raised its full-year revenue forecast. It also expects to spend $37 to $39 billion on equipment this year, and carries almost $47 billion of debt. The shares rose modestly after hours.

Take-Two rose about 8% on strong preorders for Grand Theft Auto Six. Skydance, which now owns Paramount and Warner Brothers, fell about 9% in its first report since the merger, as investors focused on $87 billion of debt.

In insurance, Gallagher Re estimates that insured catastrophe losses for the year have passed $100 billion, later than usual. Losses through September were the lowest since 2019.

## The world

A correction. Yesterday we said President Trump had paused strikes on Iran's energy plants for five days. That was wrong. That pause dates from March. On Monday, after meeting Qatar's prime minister, he said he would hold off on new strikes for now, at the request of Gulf states, while keeping the naval blockade in place. Iran's Revolutionary Guard said two more tankers were burning in the Strait of Hormuz. That is not independently confirmed.

Russian, Ukrainian and American negotiators met for about four hours in Abu Dhabi, their first three-way talks since February. Ukraine called the first day constructive, said prisoner releases are its priority, and has proposed a partial ceasefire at sea and on energy. Talks continue today.

China's exports rose 35% from a year earlier in October, and imports rose 42%. Its trade surplus this year has passed $1 trillion for the second year in a row.

## Coming up

The American bond market is closed today for Veterans Day; stocks are open. The House is expected to vote this week on impeachment articles against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, which he called a farce. On Thursday, Britain reports third-quarter growth, Munich Re reports full results, and Cisco and Applied Materials report after the close. Producer prices follow on Friday. That is the briefing. Have a good day.
